The Origin

A chef walked into a locker room

DJ spent years cooking at the highest level of professional sport. Long days, elite athletes, no margin for error. And every single day he watched the same thing happen: bodies worth millions of dollars being fuelled by food nobody actually wanted to eat.

The nutrition was technically correct. Macros counted, portions weighed, boxes ticked. But it was joyless — grey protein, steamed vegetables, the same three meals on rotation until athletes stopped eating properly altogether and reached for whatever was closest instead.

Nobody performs on food they dread. Nutrition only works if you actually eat it.

DJ — Founder, MADE

So he built the thing that should have existed already. Restaurant-calibre cooking, engineered for performance, delivered to the people doing the work — teams, schools, and anyone across the GTA who takes their training seriously enough to take their food seriously too.

The Kitchen Behind It

Where the standard came from

MADE didn't start with a business plan. It started with years of standing in kitchens where getting it wrong wasn't an option.

  1. 01 Fine dining

    The Line

    Long services where a plate leaves the pass correct or it doesn't leave at all. No shortcuts, and a standard set by whoever was hardest to please that night. Everything MADE does structurally comes from here.

  2. 02 Pro sport

    The Locker Room

    Cooking for the Toronto Raptors and the New York Knicks meant feeding rosters through a full season — back-to-backs, travel days, and the stretch in February when appetite is the first thing to go.

  3. 03 On tour

    The Course

    On the PGA Tour, players eat on a clock set by tee times rather than hunger, and a meal that sits badly costs strokes. Different sport, same lesson: food that ignores taste quietly stops being eaten.

Today

All of it, pointed at athletes

Restaurant discipline applied to performance food. Every MADE menu is built by someone who has stood in a professional kitchen at 5 a.m. and knows the difference between a meal that hits its macros and a meal an athlete finishes.

Let's Be Clear

What MADE is not

The category is crowded and the labels get sloppy. Three things we're regularly mistaken for, and one thing we actually are.

  • Not

    Not a meal kit

    Nothing arrives raw and nothing needs assembling. No recipe cards, no chopping, no sink full of pans at 9 p.m. The cooking is finished before it reaches you.

  • Not

    Not a restaurant

    There's no dining room and no menu you order off on a whim. Meals are planned against a training week, not against an appetite on a Friday night.

  • Not

    Not a supplement brand

    Nothing here comes in a tub or a sachet. We don't sell powder to pour into water — we sell food, cooked by chefs, on a plate.

What it actually is

A professional kitchen that cooks for athletes

Chef-prepared performance nutrition, built to a training standard and delivered across the Greater Toronto Area — to teams, to schools, and to anyone who trains like it matters.

Where We're Going

  1. 01
